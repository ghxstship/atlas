# ADR 0063: Shift Templates, Members' Availability and the Crew Drag Board

- Status: Proposed
- Date: 2026-10-28
- Decider: A132 (RRR-194), for the orchestrator's review
- Addresses: trace rows S4.5.6-25 (Org settings 25 Scheduling) and S11.11-01 (the crew fill part); Section 11.13
- Migrations: 2250, 2251, 2252 and 2253 (range 2250 to 2259)

## Context

Section 4.5.6 item 25 lists shift templates, open-shift claiming, swap rules and availability under Scheduling. A76 (migration 1322) made claiming and swapping take effect; templates and availability had nothing behind them. Section 11.11 asks that crew fill be quick: planners staff a week from the people most likely to take each shift and see how much is staffed.

Availability calendars existed (0740) for Gateway people only: a person's own calendar, or one "kept for one org's bookings" (`org_id` set; ADR 0019 item 10, accepted as person-owned rows by ADR 0033 decision 69). Planners in the org could read one only through an application or an open engagement.

## Decision

1. **Templates are an org table, not a setting.** `app.shift_templates` holds a name (unique in the org, ignoring case), a canon role or none, call and wrap times without a date, a headcount of 1 to 50 and a scheduled break shorter than the shift. A wrap at or before the call ends the next day, so overnight load-outs need no second field. Members read them; holders of `org.scheduling.write` (owner and admin) write them, from a panel on Settings, Scheduling (`contributions/a132.tsx`). Migration 2250 registers the table with Trash itself (`private.sync_trash_tables`, ADR 0110), so a removed template is recoverable at once rather than after the nightly sweep (pgTAP 2250 checks 2250's own recorded statements for the sync, since 2390 syncs again later); shifts already planned do not point back at a template, so removing one changes nothing planned.
2. **Planning from a template is one RPC.** `app.create_shifts_from_template(template, project, days)` needs `crew.shift.write` on the project and plans `headcount` open Draft shifts per day, at most 31 days, with the template's role and break, reading the times in the project's zone. It creates all of them or none. The shifts are ordinary open shifts: publishing, claiming and swapping keep the 1322 rules unchanged.
3. **Members' availability lives on the calendar they keep for the org.** No new table: a member's periods go on their `availability_calendars` row with `org_id` = the org, opened by `app.ensure_member_availability_calendar` (members only), and stated from Me, Work details. `private.availability_shared_with_me` (redefined from 0740) adds one clause: while the owner holds an active membership, the org's planners (`crew.shift.write` on any of its projects) read that calendar and its periods, whatever its visibility. A calendar kept for one org is that org's to see, and for such a calendar an active membership counts as sharing it with the org, as an application or an open engagement does under Section 4.8.3's "Private": the owner opened the calendar for that org's bookings and works for the org. This is a change for a Gateway person who keeps a Private calendar for an org and then joins it: from the day their membership starts, that org's planners read the calendar's periods and kinds (never its notes), and they stop when the membership ends. Visibility keeps governing general calendars (`org_id` null), and calendars kept for an org whose owner is not an active member of it, as before. Notes stay the owner's; planners read only period and kind (`api_external.availability`, `app.rank_shift_candidates`).
4. **The availability window is three org settings** (`packages/schemas/settings/a132.yaml`): `scheduling.availability_required` (off by default), `scheduling.availability_notice_days` (7) and `scheduling.availability_horizon_days` (28). While required, a member's periods that start inside the notice period are locked against adding, changing and removing (`availability_inside_notice`, trigger on `availability_blocks`); notes stay editable and the platform is not refused. The lock has no override inside the product: availability rows stay person-owned (ADR 0033 decision 69), so only the member writes them (0740), and nobody signed in, a planner included, can change a period inside the notice period. A member who cannot work a locked day tells the planner, who reassigns the shift (crew.shift.write); the refusal, the availability prompt and the Settings help say exactly that, and none of them promises a scheduler override. The horizon is how far ahead members state it: `app.my_availability_status` lists the unstated days between the notice and the horizon for the member, and the ranking marks a member overdue when nothing is stated for a shift inside the horizon. The settings are organization-scoped only, since availability belongs to the member and the org, not to one project.
5. **Ranking is in the database.** `app.rank_shift_candidates(shift, week_start)` ranks the org's active members for one shift: free of an overlapping live shift first, then stated availability over the shift (Available covering it, then Tentative or partly stated, then not stated, then Unavailable), then whether they worked the role before, then the fewest scheduled minutes in the schedule's week. It needs `crew.shift.write` on the shift's project. Overlap is a ranking signal, not a refusal: the schedule already marks double bookings, and claims refuse them (1322).
6. **The drag board loads on demand.** On the resource schedule, planners get a board (`components/crew/scheduling-board.tsx`, through `lazy-scheduling.tsx` and next/dynamic) with the week's open shifts and the people ranked for the chosen shift. Dragging a person onto any open shift assigns them, under `crew.shift.write`. That capability can reach some of an org's projects and not others, so the board lists only open shifts on the projects `app.my_shift_planning_projects` returns (2252, invariant 2, the rule the shift policies and the ranking apply); a shift on a project the planner only reads never appears there to fail on ranking or come back not found. Assigning is one RPC, `app.assign_open_shift` (2253), which locks the shift row as `app.claim_open_shift` and `app.decide_shift_claim` do. The board's open shifts are a snapshot, so a shift someone holds is refused (`shift_already_filled`): a shift a crew member claimed under the first-come rule, or another planner filled, since the board was read is never overwritten, and the board rereads. Without that guard, `private.guard_shift` would let the reassignment through and `t87_return_reassigned_shift` would quietly send the claimer's accepted shift back to Published for someone else. Under the approval and seniority rules a claim waits while the shift stays open, so the RPC also refuses a shift with a Requested claim (`shift_claims_waiting`): a planner who put someone else on it would leave the claims waiting with nobody to decide them (only `app.decide_shift_claim` supersedes them, and a later approval would fail with `shift_already_claimed`) and, under seniority, pass over the most senior claimant. The board therefore lists such shifts apart, under "Claims waiting", each linked to the shift where its claims are approved, rejected or awarded, so the 1322 rules hold. The person assigned must be an active member of the shift's org. The ranking warns of an overlapping shift (item 5) for the chosen shift; a person dropped on another shift is first checked against that shift's ranking, and one who is on an overlapping shift is not assigned: the board chooses that shift, so its warning shows, and announces why, and the planner can then press Assign to book them anyway. When no shift is left to drop on, the board says which it is: nothing planned this week, every shift staffed, or open shifts left only on projects the planner cannot staff or with claims waiting; from the keyboard, a planner chooses a shift and presses Assign on a person. Each assignment is announced in a live region, and the fill-rate meter (`role="meter"`, filled live shifts over live shifts) moves at once before the page rereads; live updates (ADR 0044) bring other planners' changes. Readers see the same meter, server-rendered. `/crew/shifts` is a key page, so none of the board ships with it.

## Consequences

- Section 4.5.6 item 25 is whole: templates, claiming, swaps and availability are each stored, edited from Settings, Scheduling and enforced.
- The crew fill target of Section 11.11 has its tool; a timed measurement belongs with the other timed journeys (gate 26).
- Compass still shows no availability editor; members state it in Atlas. A Compass screen can reuse `app.ensure_member_availability_calendar` and the blocks table as they are.
- A later package that wants templates to remember their shifts can add a nullable `shift_template_id` to `app.shifts` without changing anything here.

## Amendment (2026-10-28, P8): availability polls

P8 (RRR-222, migration 2853, parity gap G-124) adds a consequence. A planner (crew.shift.write on the project) asks
members and engaged freelancers for their availability on 1 to 31 project-local days (`app.send_availability_poll`,
which notifies each recipient, `availability.requested`). Answers are availability blocks on the recipient's calendar
kept for the org, one full project-local day each, tagged with the poll (`availability_blocks.availability_poll_id`,
set only by `app.answer_availability_poll`), so `app.rank_shift_candidates` reads them unchanged and a day already
inside the notice lock of decision 4 comes back unwritten. Polls, their days and recipients are for planners only, as
item 3 is: a member holding crew.shift.read reads none of them, and a recipient reads only the polls addressed to them
and their own recipient row. `app.availability_poll_results` counts every block answering the poll, and other blocks on
that calendar only where `private.availability_shared_with_me` already shows it to the planner, so a freelancer's
Private calendar stays private; notes never appear. Answering a poll shares those days with the org's planners, and
the answer form says so.

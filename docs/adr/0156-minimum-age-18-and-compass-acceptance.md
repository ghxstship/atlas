# ADR 0156: One Minimum Age of 18, Compass First Sign-In Acceptance and the AI Choice

- Status: Proposed
- Date: 2026-11-06
- Decider: AI, under the owner's instruction to choose the most future-proof, legally defensible option. No attorney has reviewed these calls.
- Addresses: legal issue 4 (RRR-248)
- Migrations: 6155 (range 6155 to 6159)
- Amends: ADR 0033 decision 54 (the minimum age of 16)

## Context

Compass recorded no acceptance and asked no age, so the Mobile EULA's liability cap, venue and Apple clauses rested on use of the app alone. The documents said a minimum age of 16, the Google Play target audience said 18 and over, the rating work expected below 16, and the AI text said 18 or older with nothing enforcing it. Compass sent receipt photos to the AI provider on capture, on an Admin's setting alone, while Apple guideline 5.1.2(i) asks for the person's explicit permission before personal data goes to a third-party AI.

## Decision

1. **One minimum age of 18 for every XOS account, in every app.** XOS is a workplace tool, and 18 is the age that fits the AI provider's terms, Google Play and children's privacy law alike. Minors at an event are records the responsible adult or organization keeps, with no account of their own.
2. **A neutral question, on the web and on the phone.** The FTC warns against a box that only invites "I am old enough". Both answers show with equal weight and the rule is stated only after someone answers under 18. A full birth date was rejected: it would collect a date we have no use for, and asking 18 or older answers the only question XOS has.
3. **Every account is asked, and the age is enforced on the server.** Both web shells' proxies send anyone without an 18 confirmation to `/auth/age` before any signed-in page or client navigation, however the account was made: an earlier 16, a provider, single sign-on or SCIM. The auth callback asks before a just-in-time single sign-on join, and the join runs once it is answered. Whatever a client does, the database holds two lines: nobody adds themselves to an org or a company account without the confirmation (a trigger on inserting into `app.memberships` and `app.account_memberships` and on accepting an account membership someone else added, so every way of joining is covered, through the shells, the frozen API or a direct call, including ways added later), and nobody reaches the AI provider without it (`app.ai_begin_request`, `app.ai_enabled_features`). A provisioning service, an Admin adding someone else and platform jobs are not the person answering, so they are not refused; that person is asked before their first page, and before they accept. The Console is not gated: its operators are platform staff.
   **Keys in the frozen API.** A service account is a person row so that its API keys have an audit identity (0922). It has no age to give and no screen to answer on, so `private.minimum_age_met` passes it, and API keys keep Aurora and everything else they could do; its org answers for it. A personal access token is the person themselves: until that person answers, `aurora.*` refuses with `ai_age_unconfirmed` and `organizations.create` and `invites.accept` refuse with `minimum_age_unconfirmed`, on the existing error path with no change of shape. That is a change in behavior for an unconfirmed human's token, and it is the rule.
4. **Compass asks before anything else.** After the second factor and before identity, downloads or push, a sign-in answers the age and accepts the Mobile EULA, plus the Worker Terms when the person holds an engagement or a roster entry, or no active membership. Answers go to the same logs web sign-up uses (`private.age_confirmations`, `app.legal_acceptances`), with version, time and person. Google, Apple and email sign-ins all pass here, since the step follows the session.
5. **The step needs the server, and Compass says why.** A person who has never passed it is told to connect. A person the server cleared before, or an install that already holds a cached identity, keeps working offline and is asked once the server answers: stopping a worker's time clock on a site with no signal does real harm, and the question comes at the first chance.
6. **A material change prompts through the existing mechanism** (`app.pending_legal_reacceptances`, migration 1383). During its 30 days of notice it is a dismissible prompt; from its effective date it holds Compass until accepted, since the license is the condition of using the app. Atlas and Gateway keep their banners, and each asks only about documents it publishes: the Mobile EULA is Compass's to ask, and Compass asks only about the Mobile EULA and the Worker Terms.
7. **Under 18 means no account, and closing one is confirmed first.** On a sign-up form nothing is created or kept. On an existing account the answer closes it, which cannot be undone, so one radio button or tap never does that alone: the person is told what closing means and confirms, or goes back. The rule can only be stated at that point, so a minor who goes back and lies is the residual risk of a neutral question.
8. **What stays after a refusal is stated plainly, and files go first.** SQL cannot delete storage objects, so `refuseMinimumAge` (`@xos/data/account-deletion`), the one way an app closes such an account, removes the person's badge photos, profile media (a public bucket read by URL, with certificate scans and portfolio files), staged profile uploads and support screenshots while they can still reach them, and a file that cannot be removed stops the closure so it can be tried again. To make that possible the owner of a support screenshot may now delete it, which 1034 did not allow; it costs support nothing it was owed once the person is gone. Nothing is removed from a person who has confirmed 18 or from an account that is held. The sign-in goes, and with it what XOS itself holds about them: personal acceptances (sign-in id and address), age answers, AI choices, cookie choices and opt-out signals made while signed in, support requests and feedback, personal settings and work details. With no org behind the account the person row goes too, and no deletion record stays. If some other reference still holds the row, it stays with no name and the refusal records Account Closed. With org records the account is deleted as `app.delete_my_account` does: each org keeps the records the law requires, a nameless person row, its own acceptances, and an erasure entry that holds the person's email address (or, for a sign-in with no address, a stand-in the entry says is one), and `private.account_deletions` keeps the ids. `private.age_refusals` adds one row: surface, month and outcome, under a random id, because a time-ordered id or an exact time could be matched to that deletion. The address given to a sign-up form is used only for the limit on that form, as a hash pruned within a day. A person is counted once, so a repeat answer on a held account adds no row, and neither does closing it later. Backups keep deleted data until they expire, and the words the person is shown say all of this.
9. **An account that cannot close yet is not left working.** A legal hold, an open shift or being an org's only owner stops deletion. Every session ends, the person is told why and sent to Legal Contact, they are asked again at every sign-in and page, and a repeat answer closes the account once nothing blocks it. Until then they hold no 18 confirmation, so they join nothing and reach no AI. `private.age_refusal_holds` names them for the platform team, since XOS still holds their account and has to act on what they said. If they later answer 18 or older the row stays, so the conflict stays visible and a person decides.
10. **A confirmation at 16 stays as evidence and no longer counts.** The proxies ask those people again at their next page; Compass asks at its next launch.
11. **The AI choice is the person's, and a decline binds on the server.** The first time an AI feature would send anything in Compass, it names the provider category and the provider, says what goes to it, and records the choice with the version of the words shown. `app.ai_begin_request` refuses a person who declined and `app.ai_enabled_features` answers nothing, whichever app asks, so saying no in Compass is honored everywhere. Compass sends nothing of the person's without Allowed, and the choice can be changed in Settings. The words are scoped to what that holds, and they say the rest: Atlas does not ask yet, so a person with no recorded choice keeps the org's setting there, and the choice covers the features a person uses themselves, so a coworker's AI feature can still send records about them that the coworker can see. Stopping that would take a per-subject rule the AI features do not have. The version of the words changed with them (`2026-11-06.2`).
12. **Nobody has to agree to leave.** The age step offers Sign Out and Delete Account beside its two answers, and declining the documents, first time or after a material change has taken effect, offers Review Again, Sign Out and Delete Account (guideline 5.1.1(v)). So someone who neither agrees nor says they are under 18 can delete the account from inside Compass. The ordinary deletion keeps its usual records.

## Alternatives rejected

- **Guardian assent for 16 and 17 year olds.** It adds a consent flow, a second identity to verify and a disaffirmance risk, for a workplace tool that has no need of it.
- **Keeping a hash of the refused email to stop a second attempt.** It would keep an identifier for the person the rule exists to protect. The FTC's suggestion against changing an answer is a device-level one, which matters most for a child-directed audience, and XOS has none.
- **Blocking offline use for anyone who has not answered.** See decision 5.
- **A session cookie to skip the age read in the proxy.** It would add a Cookie Policy entry to save one read; the proxies already read the person on each request.
- **Recording the AI choice only on the device.** A choice that cannot be shown later and does not bind the server is not worth much.

## Consequences

- The Terms, Privacy Policy, Mobile EULA, Worker Terms, AUP and AI Features Notice must say 18 and describe these steps.
- Store answers change: see `apps/compass/store/age-rating.md`.
- `app.opportunities.minimum_age` still allows 16 and 17, and the frozen API documents a minimum of 16. A posting cannot reach anyone under 18 now that every account is 18 or older, so it is left alone until a later API version.
- Every existing web account without an 18 confirmation is asked once, at its next page, and joins nothing and reaches no AI until it answers.
- Limits, stated so the documents do not overstate them: the question is a self-declaration and is not verified. The proxies do not stop a server action from a page already open, and a direct API or database client with a valid token can still read and write what its role allows; only joining and AI are refused without the answer. The Compass acceptance of the Mobile EULA is enforced by the app, not the server, and a kiosk punch by PIN does not pass the gate. Compass's screens are tested in a DOM, not on a device: a simulator run of `first-sign-in-acceptance` and `first-sign-in-under-18` is a release condition.
- The platform team reads `private.age_refusal_holds` directly until the Console has a page for it. Closing a held account by hand has to remove its files too, as `refuseMinimumAge` does.
- Demo seeds answer for the demo people, so journeys that sign in as them start on their own screens.

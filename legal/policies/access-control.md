# Access Control Policy

> **Draft for review by counsel before launch.** This internal policy is a draft. It takes effect when the Owner approves it, and its effective date is that date of approval.

## 1. Purpose and Scope

This policy governs who may access GHXSTSHIP Industries LLC ("GHXSTSHIP") systems and data, and how XOS 4.0 controls access to Customer Data. It covers personnel access to internal systems, cloud consoles, the repository and production, and the access model built into the product. It implements the Information Security Policy.

## 2. Principles

- **Least privilege.** Grant the minimum access needed for the task, for the shortest time.
- **Need to know.** Access to Confidential and Restricted data requires a business reason.
- **Separation of duties.** No one person can both make and approve a sensitive change.
- **Deny by default.** An absent grant is a refusal. The database refuses access unless a policy allows it.

## 3. Personnel Access to Internal Systems

3.1 **Identity.** Every person has a unique named account. Shared accounts are prohibited, except break-glass accounts held in the secrets vault and audited on every use.

3.2 **Authentication.** Multi-factor authentication is required on every system that supports it: the source host, the cloud consoles (Supabase, Vercel, Expo, Stripe, Twilio, Resend, Sentry, Anthropic, Checkr), email and the password manager. Phishing-resistant factors (passkeys or security keys) are required for production and administrative access. Passwords are unique, generated by the password manager, and at least 15 characters where a password is used.

3.3 **Provisioning.** Access is requested through a ticket naming the system, role and reason, and approved by the system owner. Production and administrative access also needs the Security Lead's approval.

3.4 **Deprovisioning.** Access is removed on the last day of work, or immediately on involuntary departure. Shared secrets the person could reach are rotated.

3.5 **Access reviews.** System owners review every account and role each quarter. The Security Lead keeps the evidence. Unjustified access is removed within 5 business days.

3.6 **Privileged access.** Production database access is limited to named people, granted just in time for a stated task, logged, and expires within 8 hours. Routine operations use migrations and stored procedures, not ad hoc queries. Superuser and service-role keys live only in Supabase Vault and Vercel encrypted environment variables.

3.7 **Source code.** Commits to `main` are signed and arrive only through reviewed pull requests that pass CI. Branch protection prevents force pushes and deletion.

3.8 **AI agents.** AI coding agents work in isolated worktrees, edit only the paths they own, never hold production credentials, and cannot merge to `main`. Their output is reviewed like any contributor's.

## 4. Access to Customer Data by Personnel

- Personnel never view Customer Data in the Service without a support session granted by the Customer's Owner. Sessions expire within 24 hours and every action is written to that Customer's audit ledger.
- Partner organizations reach client data only through a support session or a standing grant recorded with scope and expiry.
- Access without a support session is allowed only when the law compels it or to prevent imminent harm, needs the Owner's approval, and is reported to the Customer unless the law forbids it.

## 5. Access Control in the Product

The XOS platform enforces these rules in Postgres:

- **Roles.** Platform roles per organization (Owner, Admin, Manager, Member, Collaborator, Field, Viewer), project roles that narrow scope, and custom roles built from capabilities.
- **Capabilities.** Fine-grained verbs checked in row-level security through `private.has_capability`.
- **Time-boxed access.** Memberships and grants carry valid-from and valid-to dates enforced in SQL.
- **Separation of duties.** Default rules that organizations may tighten but never remove: a purchase order's creator cannot approve it; whoever creates a vendor or edits its bank details cannot approve payment to it; the submitter of an expense, timesheet or time-off request cannot approve it; a journal entry's preparer cannot post it; no one can grant themselves a role or capability; a change order's requester cannot price and approve it alone.
- **Spend authority.** Approval limits by role or person, document type, GL scope and amount, enforced inside the approval stored procedures.
- **Field-level permissions.** Restricted columns are masked unless the caller holds the matching capability.
- **External access.** External users reach rows only through their engagements, their account's engagements or a representation, through column-allowlisted views, and only while the engagement's state allows.
- **Transparency.** View as role, Why can I see this, quarterly access reviews with optional automatic removal, and one-hour break-glass access with MFA, a written reason and notice to every Admin.
- **Authentication.** Breached-password checks, MFA by role, configurable session lifetime, step-up authentication for billing, export, role changes and API key creation, IP allow-lists, SAML SSO and SCIM.
- **Non-human access.** Service accounts with capability-scoped, expiring API keys and their own audit identity. OAuth apps with scopes that map one to one to capability groups.

Every rule above has a pgTAP test, and a cross-tenant attack suite runs on every table in every build.

# Data Retention and Deletion Policy

> **Draft before launch.** This document is a draft. It is not in effect and no one may rely on it. Showing it at /legal does not put it in effect. It takes effect only on the effective date that GHXSTSHIP Industries LLC states at the top of a final version.

This policy states how long XOS 4.0 keeps data, how deletion works, and how Customers control retention for the data they own. GHXSTSHIP Industries LLC ("GHXSTSHIP") applies it as processor for Customer Data and as controller for its own records.

## 1. Principles

- **Purpose-bound, with limits we name.** Each period below is set from a purpose, a legal obligation or a Customer instruction. Not every row has an end date, and the next two points say which do not.
- **Enforced by the database.** Retention policies are recorded in `retention_policies`. A scheduled job (pg_cron) runs nightly and deletes rows those policies make due. A nightly sweep gives any table that can be soft-deleted, and any append-only ledger, the general rule below if it has no other policy. The job removes database rows. Section 4 says what happens to stored files.
- **The general rule.** Where the schedule says "general rule", a record stays until a person with permission deletes it or the organization's data is purged. A deleted record moves to Trash and is purged 7 years after it was deleted. Until then it can be restored, so a delete under this rule is not an erasure. An append-only ledger is purged 7 years after each entry was created. People rows are the exception: a person whose account is deleted keeps a row with no full name, and no purge removes it, so that records naming that row stay consistent.
- **Periods set but not yet applied by a job.** Under the general rule, a record nobody deletes has no end date. GHXSTSHIP has set periods for its own records that the general rule would otherwise leave open: support tickets 3 years after they close, billing records 7 years, acceptance and age records for the life of the account and 7 years after it, IP addresses in those records removed after 12 months, Work Details in an account with no sign-in for 3 years deleted after notice, and punch coordinates 3 years. No scheduled job applies these yet. GHXSTSHIP applies them by 12 months after launch at the latest, and the schedule below marks each one.
- **After an answer of under 18.** When a person answers that they are under 18 and confirms it, their personal acceptances, age answers, cookie choices, opt-out signals made while signed in, support requests, feedback and support screenshots are deleted rather than kept for the periods below, and so are their AI choices. Acceptances given on an organization's behalf stay with that organization.
- **Legal hold wins.** An active legal hold in an organization suspends the nightly purge and the organization purge for that organization, and refuses an account deletion that would change the held records, until the hold is released. A hold is released, never edited or deleted, and placing and releasing it is logged.
- **GHXSTSHIP's hold during a return.** GHXSTSHIP also places a legal hold on an organization while it returns data the export omits (Terms 11.2, DPA 10.2). That hold covers every table, holds the deletion and the purge, and is released when the return is done, refused or withdrawn.
- **Customer control.** Admins can see the retention policies and classifications in Settings, Data and Privacy, which are read-only. A Customer can set two periods today: how long chat messages are kept, and how long project facts are kept. It cannot shorten any other period, including the AI log. A Customer can delete a record at any time, which moves it to Trash. The Service has no action that empties Trash or permanently deletes a single record, so erasure before the purge date needs a written request to GHXSTSHIP (Section 3). A Customer can use a legal hold or an export to keep records longer.
- **One schedule.** These periods are the same everywhere and do not change by jurisdiction by themselves. A Customer whose law requires a longer period is responsible for keeping those records, using a legal hold or an export.

## 3. Deletion Events

**Record deletion.** Deleting a record moves it to Trash. It can be restored until its purge date, then its row is purged. Section 4 covers its files. The Service has no action that empties Trash or purges a single record sooner.

**Account deletion by a person.** A person can delete their account in Compass (More, Settings, Delete Account) or in Gateway (Settings, Account), and the page at /legal/account-deletion explains this and the route for someone who cannot sign in. It runs at once. Deletion is refused while the person is clocked in, or is the only Owner or Account Owner, or while an organization they belong to or work for has a legal hold over the kinds of records a deletion changes, even if the hold is not about them. That page gives the route for someone whose deletion is refused. Their profile and its files, sign-in, personal settings, Work Details and AI choices are erased, their memberships end and their open engagements are canceled. A person who answers that they are under 18 is closed the same way once they confirm it, after passing any second factor their sign-in holds, and the records listed under After an answer of under 18 in Section 1 are deleted too. Their full name is removed from their person row. Separate given and family names are not yet removed, including those Sign in with Apple can set, so the person should clear them first. Each organization they worked with keeps records such as timesheets, payments, tax documents and engagement records, and receives an erasure request in its register. The records keep a person with no name, though a tax form keeps the legal name on it and a signed agreement keeps the signed name. Records they created for an organization stay without their name.

**Ending a paid plan.** Choosing End plan in Settings, Billing and Plan, or ending the plan in the Stripe portal, ends it at the end of the paid period and moves the organization to the Access plan. A subscription that Stripe ends after failed payments also moves the organization to the Access plan. Nothing is deleted. An organization on the Access plan stays until its Owner cancels or deletes it, and nothing removes it for lack of use.

**Organization cancellation.** When the Owner cancels the organization in Settings, Danger Zone, it becomes read-only at once, and any paid subscription ends in Stripe at once. It can be reactivated for 30 days, and the Owner can export its data. At day 90 its data is deleted from the live service, backups age out on their schedule, and the Owner receives a deletion certificate. A legal hold, or a purge that would touch another organization's records, delays this until it is resolved.

**Organization deletion.** The Owner deletes with step-up authentication and by typing the organization name. It becomes read-only at once. A recovery window runs for the rest of that day plus 7 full days. The purge then runs as for cancellation, without waiting for day 90, and only once Stripe has accepted the cancellation of any paid subscription, so the Owner must export within the window. A partner with client organizations cannot cancel or delete, and a legal hold refuses deletion.

**Data subject erasure.** A Customer tracks erasure requests in the data request register in Settings, Data and Privacy, with a 30-day clock. The register records the request and its outcome. It does not itself delete data across tables. The Customer's staff can correct records and delete them with the Service's tools, honoring legal holds and legal minimums, but a delete moves the record to Trash, and its attached files and any AI log entries that quote it stay. The Service offers no anonymize or permanent delete action. Permanent erasure sooner than the purge date, including the Trash copy, attached files and AI log entries, is done by GHXSTSHIP on the Customer's written instruction to the privacy mailbox under Section 8.3 of the Data Processing Agreement. GHXSTSHIP responds within 10 business days of the instruction, so a Customer should ask early enough to meet its own 30-day clock. Business days are Monday to Friday, except US federal holidays, in New York time. This is the route for people who have no account too, including guests and people named in records. An account holder's own deletion, above, adds a Completed erasure request automatically.

## 4. Deletion Methods

- Database rows are deleted, or anonymized where a record must remain for integrity, such as a ledger entry.
- **Stored files.** Files are deleted from Storage, with their versions, when an organization's data is purged, when an upload fails its malware scan, and when a person deletes their account for their profile media and badge photos. The nightly purge of a single record removes its row and does not yet remove a file attached to it. Until that is built, that file remains in Storage until the organization's data is purged. A raw inbound email is such a file.
- Field-level encrypted values become unrecoverable once the row holding them is purged and the backups holding it have expired.
- Backups are not edited. Deleted data expires from backups on their schedule, and is not restored to the live service except as part of a full disaster recovery. We have not yet written a step that re-applies deletions after such a restore, so a restore could bring back deleted data until we erase it again.
- A deletion certificate records the organization, the counts of what was deleted, when, and that backups age out on their own schedule. It does not give a backup expiry date. It covers Customer Data, not the billing evidence GHXSTSHIP keeps as controller (Section 2).

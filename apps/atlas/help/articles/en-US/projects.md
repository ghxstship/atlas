A project holds one production from first idea to close. Its tabs bring together the schedule, work, people, places and money that belong to it.

## Find a project

**All projects** lists every project you can see. **By phase** groups them by their current phase, and **Portfolio timeline** shows their dates side by side. Filter by state or search by name or key.

## Create a project

Choose **New project**, give it a name, a project key and a time zone, and pick its venue and jurisdiction if you know them. The key prefixes every record of the project, such as NWL-42.

## Inside a project

- **Overview** shows the phase, the next gate and what is at risk.
- **Gates** shows the criteria each gate needs. See [Gates](help:gates).
- **Scope** is the tree of shows, sites and workstreams.
- Module tabs, such as Schedule, Crew or Logistics, show that module filtered to the project.

Project settings hold the name, dates, currency and time zone. Some settings are locked by your organization; the page shows where a locked value comes from.

## Event types and the staged intake

Your organization can describe the kinds of event it produces in **Settings, Templates, Event types**: a festival, a gala, a trade show. Each event type picks the template a new project starts from by its scale (Intimate up to 500 people, Small 500 to 2,500, Medium 2,500 to 10,000, Large 10,000 to 50,000, Major over 50,000) and its audience, asks for its own project details at intake, and names the documents a project needs by a gate.

When your organization has event types, **New project** walks through steps: **Start** (the event type, and whether to start from its template, another template, a past project or blank), **Scale and audience**, **Basics** (the key, name, show dates, time zone and venue, with the load-in and load-out days under More fields), **Details** (the type's fields and its guidance), **Copy** (only when starting from a past project) and **Review**, which names the template that will be applied and what the type requires. Create makes the project in one step; if anything is refused, nothing is created. Without event types, New project stays a single screen.

## Show days, bump-in and bump-out

The first and last show days are the project's start and end dates. Load-in and load-out days are project days on the schedule; the days around the show give the **bump-in** and **bump-out** windows, which the Overview lists, the dock schedule shades and procurement compares hires with. Atlas warns, without refusing, when a load-in falls after the first show day, a load-out before the last, the windows overlap or one is longer than 60 days.

## Copy from another project

In **Project settings**, **Copy from a project** copies the plan, pass categories, hotel blocks, budgets and advance forms of a past project into this one, moved by a number of days (filled in from the two first show days). Times keep their local time, also across a daylight saving change. Preview shows every item with what is copied and what is skipped and why; Copy writes exactly that. Shifts, people, engagements, credentials and the answers of the companies you engage are never copied.
